On the Subject of Wire Cutting

This module contains 4 wires of various colours.

Only one wire needs to be cut to solve the module. Wires are ordered from top to bottom.

Follow the list of rules to determine which wire to cut:

  • If there is exactly one red wire and the last digit of the serial number is odd, cut the 2nd wire.
  • Otherwise, if the last wire is blue, cut the 4th wire.
  • Otherwise, if there is at least one white wire, cut the first white wire.
  • Otherwise, cut the 2nd wire.

On the Subject of Switches

All switches begin in the DOWN position. Toggling a switch refers to moving it from the DOWN position to the UP position or vice versa.

There are 4 switches, numbered from left to right. Follow the list of rules from top to bottom three times to determine the final switch positions.

  1. If the serial number has an odd last digit, toggle the first and third switch.
  2. If the number of batteries is not 0, toggle the fourth switch.
  3. If the first and fourth switches are in the same position, toggle the second switch.
  4. Change the position of the first switch to the opposite of the fourth switch.
  5. Toggle the first and third switch.

When the switches are in the correct final position, press the submit button to solve the module.

Submitting incorrect positions will result in a strike.

On the Subject of Binary Flashes

  • The 5 LEDs represent a 5 bit binary number. The LED farthest left represents the most significant bit.
  • Each binary number is an entry in one of eight numeric sequences below. The sequences travel left to right, wrapping around when reaching the end.
  • Two numbers are highlighted in either red, blue or green. Press the corresponding coloured button when the LED pattern matches one of the highlighted numbers. Pressing the wrong button will result in a strike.
  • Press all three buttons to solve the module.

Decrypting the message

  • Create a 5x5 grid of letters. Start with your key and fill the rest of the grid with the unused letters of the alphabet (excluding J).
  • Split the message displayed on the module into letter pairs. For each pair, find them in the grid:
    • If the letters are in the same row, replace them with the letters to their immediate left (wrapping around to the right if needed).
    • If the letters are in the same column, replace them with the letters immediately above (wrapping around to the bottom if needed).
    • If the letters are in different rows and columns, replace each letter with the letter in its own row and the column of the other letter.
  • Combine the resulting letters to form the decrypted message. 'J' and 'I' are considered the same letter.
